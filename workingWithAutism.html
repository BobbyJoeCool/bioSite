Understanding and Working with Asperger's (Autism)

Asperger's, now under the Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) umbrella, is a neurological disorder commonly associated with difficulties with social communication and understanding social cues, repetitive patterns of motions (stimming behaviors) such as flapping arms, being highly focuses on single areas of interests, and relying strongly on routines to regulate their emotions. This creates some challenges in life and work. People with this type of disorder are commonly called neurodivergent (ND), where people without neurological disorders are referred to as neurotypical (NT). Being less aware of social cues means needing to rely on an "internal dictionary" of known meanings and being more consciously aware where a NT person is more subconsciously able to see and understand these things.

For Josh, the fixation on single areas of interest, and the needing of routines have helped him in his forging adventures. If you watch shows like Forged in Fire, they edit out all the boring and tedious aspects of forging. Many people who want to get into the craft do not realize how much time and patience it takes and lose their interest fast. However, this aspect of ASD has really pushed Josh to keep learning and stick with the routine. Josh has even said there is something therapeutic about the rhythmic motions of hammering a piece of iron.

All of this being said, ASD has other levels that can be more debilitating. While many, like Josh, can live seemingly normal lives while just seeming a little quirky, some are unable to care for themselves at all. While it means that people with Level 1, or High-Functioning, are able to "blend in" to society with little issues, it doesn't mean that it isn't difficult. The biggest issue with awareness is that Level 1 ASD is what is known as an invisible disability. This means that an average NT person would not be able to identify the disability the way you can spot a person with a broken leg or Tourette. This makes awareness difficult, which is turn makes accommodation difficult. This is why most awareness campaigns focus on slogans like "Be Kind to Everyone."

If you are interested in learning more about Autism Spectrum Disorder, or spreading awareness, below are some links to resources, and a favorite seller of Josh's.

Autism Speaks

Autism Speaks is a U.S.-based nonprofit focused on autism awareness, research, advocacy, and family support. It provides educational resources such as toolkits for newly diagnosed individuals, state-by-state service guides, early screening information, and a national helpline to connect families with local support. Through community programs and public education efforts, the organization works to increase understanding and improve access to services across the lifespan.

Spark for Autism

SPARK for Autism is a large research initiative that aims to advance understanding of autism through genetic and behavioral studies. It connects autistic individuals and families with researchers, offering opportunities to participate in studies and contribute to scientific discoveries. The program also provides educational resources and shares research updates to help families stay informed about developments in autism science.

Autism Society of America

Autism Society of America is a long-standing U.S. nonprofit that provides education, advocacy, support, and community resources for autistic individuals and their families. The organization offers information on autism characteristics, screening and services, local support networks, and practical guidance on topics like school, work, and healthcare.

Be Kind to Everyone

Be Kind to Everyone is a family-run apparel brand founded to help Jordyn, a young woman with autism, build real-world job skills and independence. What began as a small summer project has grown into a meaningful business where she helps package and ship products that promote kindness and inclusion. The brand highlights how individuals with autism can be productive, capable, and empowered through opportunity and support.
